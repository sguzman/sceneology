# Scene semantics: initial public vocabulary

A **scene** is an addressable composition of elements whose meaning or presentation depends on one or more spatial, temporal, visual, auditory, behavioral or interaction relationships.

A **scene element** is an object, character, environment feature, emitter, camera, light, controller or other participant.

A **relation** connects elements through containment, adjacency, support, attachment, occlusion, relative pose, timing, interaction, causality or other typed semantics.

A **constraint** limits valid scene states or realizations: size, count, placement, occupancy, material, behavior, timing, compatibility, continuity and similar rules.

A **style** specifies an aesthetic or representational grammar without changing the underlying identity of the represented objects.

A **realization** binds the description to an actual implementation: authored art, constructive geometry, procedural generation, rigged objects, audio synthesis, compositing, a runtime or a hybrid.

A **scene variant** is an identifiable derivation or configuration of a scene. It may be an experiment, failure, study or independently approved gallery exhibit.

A **rendering** is observable output from a realization at a specified camera/time/state and renderer configuration; a still image alone is not proof of interactive behavior.

This is deliberately a compact starting vocabulary. Precise typed grammars, JSON/YAML examples, technical invariants and object-family ontologies are future modules subject to review.
